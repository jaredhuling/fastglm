Analytic inference for partial cumulative-link models
Development specification, baseline 351d808, 2026-11-04

Scientific target
-----------------
Test a specified linear hypothesis L theta = h within an identified partial
cumulative-link model. Examples include a zero common effect, a particular
threshold-specific effect, or equality of one predictor's slopes across all
thresholds while other predictors remain nonparallel. A zero-effect test and
a common-slope test are different hypotheses. Parameters and frequency weights
retain the definitions validated in partial-proportional-odds-validation/.

Maximum-likelihood inference
----------------------------
The ordinary likelihood is optimized in the affine constraint L theta = h,
intersected with the strict row-specific probability domain. The unrestricted
and restricted likelihoods yield LR = 2(l_full-l_restricted). The chi-square
reference has rank(L) degrees of freedom under an interior true null, fixed
finite dimension, full limiting information, independent ordinal observations
or integer frequency counts, and regular consistent ML fits. Convergence is
not a proof of these assumptions or of a global maximum. Probability boundaries
are not ordinary chi-square cases. Failed optimization returns unavailable
inference; it does not turn a finite search bound into an infinite interval.

A profile confidence limit for one coefficient solves LR = chi-square(1)
quantile, with every nuisance fit constrained to remain in the probability
domain. This is ordinary likelihood inference only. The mean-bias adjustment
is not generally a gradient, so no scalar Firth penalty is invented for these
ordinal profiles.

Full-model adjusted-score inference
-----------------------------------
Let B be a full-column-rank basis of null(L) in the fitted linear coordinates,
I the full-model expected information, and U* = U+A the full-model adjusted
score. The constrained nuisance estimate solves B' U* = 0 without replacing A
by the adjustment of a smaller model. Define

 M = Identity - I B (B' I B)^(-1) B',
 e = M U*,      T = e' I^(-1) e.

For ordinary ML scores use A=0. The projection removes residual nuisance score
numerically and is also necessary when the chosen constrained nuisance rule
does not make the ordinary nuisance score zero. Algebraically M I M' equals
I - I B (B' I B)^(-1) B' I, an efficient information matrix of rank rank(L).
Whitening by I^(-1/2) makes the corresponding matrix an orthogonal projection
of rank rank(L), so a standard normal limiting score gives chi-square(rank(L)).

Under local smoothness and an interior consistent root, I=O(n), U=O_p(sqrt(n)),
A=O(1), and its local derivatives have order one. A Taylor expansion of the
projected nuisance equation gives a root-n-consistent nuisance estimate. Its
leading score shift is I B times a nuisance displacement, annihilated by M.
The adjustment contributes O(1), hence o_p(1) after score normalization by
sqrt(n). The statistic therefore has the same first-order limit as the ordinary
efficient-score statistic. This argument requires a regular fixed-dimensional
sequence with nonsingular limiting information and an appropriate score CLT;
it is not a higher-order or small-sample calibration theorem. The parameter
design may contain unrestricted threshold-specific slopes: the proof uses
only the full model, linear constraints, and interior regularity.

For an invertible constant coordinate change theta=S delta, transform L to LS,
U* to S'U*, I to S'IS, and the nuisance basis accordingly. The constrained
equations and efficient quadratic are unchanged. Linear equality contrasts
between threshold slopes are therefore valid even when other slopes remain
nonparallel. A nonlinear reparameterization has a different mean-bias target
and is outside this argument. A reduced-model meanBR fit cannot generally be
substituted for the constrained full-model adjusted root.

Feasibility and failure contract
--------------------------------
For every positive-frequency row, adjacent cumulative-predictor gaps are
 alpha_(j+1)-alpha_j - z'(gamma_(j+1)-gamma_j) > 0.
Common slopes and offsets cancel. The intersection of these linear halfspaces
and the null affine space is convex. Raw thresholds need not be ordered when
the predictor origin is outside the data domain. Feasible-start searches and
step bounds must use row-specific gaps. Zero-frequency rows do not impose
fitted-likelihood constraints. All native probability and information checks
remain active, including at a proposed constrained solution.

Assumption and implementation audit
-----------------------------------
Checked directly: declared categories and effective frequency-count semantics
come from ordinal response validation; full-rank constraints come from scaled
QR; full-model information is checked by the native evaluator; all proposed
states have positive probabilities on positive-frequency rows. Step bounds
are obtained from the row-specific linear gaps. Constraint starts remain in
the affine null space and search its intersection with those halfspaces.
Failure to find a start is reported as a failed search, not a proof of global
infeasibility. Starting margin reductions do not modify the statistical domain.

Guaranteed to numerical tolerance: row-gap sums retain original threshold and
slope coefficients, with compensated accumulation before rounding. Predictor
steps use one compensated sum across threshold, common and varying effects.
Affine projection checks representability, and final reported coefficients
are checked against the original constraints. A requested difference smaller
than representable coefficient spacing returns unavailable inference rather
than silently changing the null hypothesis. No probability clipping, scalar
penalty, pseudoinverse, degrees-of-freedom reduction or Wald fallback is used.

Taken as statistical assumptions: independence, correct model specification,
an interior true parameter, fixed dimension, nonsingular limiting information,
and consistent roots/local quadratic likelihood expansion. The fixed bounded
designs with positive probabilities and all cells sampled at growing integer
frequencies provide a concrete nondegenerate regime in which the ordinary
multinomial CLT and smooth fixed-dimensional arguments apply. Under those
conditions, expanding both constrained and unrestricted log likelihoods gives
LR equal to the ordinary efficient-score quadratic plus o_p(1). The adjusted
score argument above supplies only the same first-order reference. Arbitrary
model selection, clustered observations, increasing dimension, and probability
boundary nulls are outside this justification. Robust covariance elsewhere in
the package does not convert these likelihood-based tests into clustered tests.

Evidence and status after implementation
----------------------------------------
The pointwise development preflight passed 21 of 21 checks before enabling
partial adjusted-score inference. Independent numerical category-log-score
cumulants agreed with the native adjustment within 8.29e-10; efficient
quadratics agreed within 2.11e-9. Projection identities agreed within 2.48e-14.
These are algebraic and numerical development checks, not calibration evidence.

The fresh confirmation passed 84 of 84 prespecified comparisons across all

The nuisance equation error includes numerical differentiation and falls below

predictor-update tolerances separately.

The initial confirmation attempt is retained in confirmation-2026-11-04/.
It stopped at a script error: grouped and expanded responses used different
category labels, so a named contrast correctly failed validation. All completed
comparisons passed. The only correction copied grouped category labels to the
expanded response; no data values, hypotheses, tolerances, solvers or package
code changed. The original runner and explanation remain with that attempt.

The new partial-profile test file passes 129 expectations, including source
domain checks, equality contrasts with other slopes still nonparallel, generic
adjusted-score validation, independent profile maximization, impossible row-gap
nulls, exhausted iterations, zero-frequency rows, extreme cancellation,
unrepresentable constraints, invalid interval levels and default parameters.
Existing common-slope profile tests also pass. Production changes are limited
to generalized constrained inference and contrast dispatch; dense native model
equations are unchanged. Streaming constrained inference remains unsupported.

The matrix identities and linear-domain geometry are exact algebra. The
chi-square interpretation is conditional on the stated regularity assumptions.
The reported implementation agreements are empirical numerical evidence.
Higher-order calibration, universal finite roots and global convergence remain
unestablished; none is claimed. No inference calibration simulation was run.
