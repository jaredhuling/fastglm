Ordinal analytic inference: interpretation
==========================================

Decision
--------
The constrained ML/profile implementation and the full-model adjusted-score
procedure are ready for the supported dense ordinal interface. The independent
checks support numerical correctness across logit, probit and cloglog links,
frequency grouping, offsets, and mixed threshold/location constraints. They
do not establish finite-sample coverage or improved type-I error calibration.
The scientific scope and assumptions remain those in methods.txt.

External software comparison
-----------------------------
reference-correction-v2 contains the usable comparison results. In all six
prespecified generated cases, the unrestricted and restricted ML log
likelihoods agreed with ordinal::clm within 1.37e-12 and 9.10e-13, respectively.
Every profile endpoint was obtained. The largest absolute endpoint difference
from ordinal's independently constructed profiles was 1.82e-6; the additional
MASS housing comparison, across all three links and two slopes, differed by
at most 1.31e-6. These are much smaller than the protocol's 5e-4 endpoint
tolerance. The comparison uses ordinal 2023.12-4.1 on the recorded R session.

The largest absolute likelihood-ratio target error at a generated-data
endpoint was 8.93e-7. All reported nuisance scores and remaining predictor
steps met their 1e-7 convergence criteria. Across the 12 ordinary/adjusted
joint score checks, constraint residuals were at most 4.45e-16, and invertible
row transformations changed the statistic by at most 2.85e-13. No method
failure or fitting warning occurred in the corrected six-case panel.

Harness correction and evidence history
----------------------------------------
confirmation-v1 is retained intact as a failed comparison attempt. Its
likelihood and score records were successful, but its endpoint comparisons
were invalid: ordinal::confint.clm ignores its parm argument and returned all
slopes, which the original harness recycled against the selected fastglm
coefficient. The returned message identified this behavior. The correction
selects the requested rows from ordinal's result explicitly. No fastglm
algorithm, tolerance, null hypothesis, dataset, or seed was changed in
response. reference-correction-v2 reuses the same saved-seed inputs to confirm
the corrected reference comparison; it is not a new independent sample.
Both directories preserve their runner and protocol, source hashes, metadata,
complete numeric records and generated inputs. The top-level run.R contains
the corrected runner. A subsequent roxygen wording change clarifies the local
smoothness/consistency assumptions without changing the tested calculation.

Development regression checks
------------------------------
tests/testthat/test-ordinal-profile.R additionally checks nested likelihoods,
an independent direct likelihood optimized by nlminb, exact numerical
inversion of the scalar binomial likelihood ratio, hand-derived binary score
quadratics, full-model versus smaller-model nuisance adjustments, grouping
and affine recoding invariance, a finite score null under a separated
alternative, and explicit nonconvergence/empty-category/streaming failures.
These tests are implementation regression checks rather than asymptotic
theorem verification or a coverage experiment.

Limits and next step
---------------------
Profile intervals currently require a converged full ML fit, retained dense
data, and positive observed frequency for every declared category. Score
tests require a converged supplied fit and a numerically usable constrained
null. A finite mean-bias-reduced fit does not establish those conditions.
Nuisance fitting or endpoint-search failure stays unavailable; an exhausted
search is never described as an infinite confidence limit. Score-profile
confidence sets remain deferred until their possibly nonmonotone geometry
can be handled explicitly. The natural next inference extension is source-
based constrained evaluation, preserving the same full-model equations and
failure semantics, rather than a simulation-calibrated adjustment.
