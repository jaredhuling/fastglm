Ordinal source covariance: validation results
============================================

Baseline: 17c4a8a. This is an implementation audit of established covariance
calculations, not a new estimator or a finite-sample coverage claim. The exact
formulas, assumptions, source contract, storage bounds, and reproduction command
are in methods.txt.

Implemented behavior
--------------------
Common and partial ordinal source fits now support HC0, HC1, and one-way cluster
covariance through fastglm_vcov() and vcov(). One source reread accumulates the
ordinary category scores and observed negative Hessians. Full threshold/slope
cross-covariances and the established meanBR first-order convention are retained.

Cluster callbacks receive the block index and return labels in original block
row order. Exact matching joins clusters across blocks without collapsing numeric
labels that print identically. Labels missing only at zero-frequency rows are
allowed. Only distinct positive-frequency labels and one score vector per such
cluster are retained; additional cluster storage is O(Gd).

No full design, response, or observation-level score matrix is collected by the
covariance implementation. The user-supplied source may itself retain data; the
bounded-memory contract requires an appropriate reader. Numerical source values
must remain unchanged between reads, as required for the fitted source itself.

Regression evidence
-------------------
The focused and existing regression run passed 739 expectations with zero
failures, errors, skips, or test warnings. Its retained log is
validation-20261104/tests.log. Files exercised:

  tests/testthat/test-ordinal-inference.R
  tests/testthat/test-ordinal-partial-robust.R
  tests/testthat/test-ordinal-source-covariance.R
  tests/testthat/test-ordinal-streaming.R

The new checks cover common/partial slopes, all three links, ML/meanBR,
dense/prepared/source representations, grouped/expanded frequencies, HC0/HC1,
clusters crossing blocks, full-precision labels, all-zero blocks, original-row
callback alignment, factor labels, formula coding, reread/schema errors,
supplied-covariance summaries, and extreme raw-count/frequency products.

The older housing-source fixture removes a declared category through its
weights. Its covariance rejection remains correct and is now tested as an
empty-category restriction in both dense and source representations, rather
than as a blanket prohibition on source covariance.

Independent audit
-----------------
All 120 checks in validation-20261104/checks.csv passed:

  48 comparisons establish bit-for-bit agreement of the refactored dense
     aggregation with its saved baseline source, including covariance metadata.
  48 comparisons establish source/dense agreement for HC0/HC1, with and without
     clustering, across both slope structures, all links, and both estimators.
  24 comparisons use independently differentiated category log probabilities
     and the total ordinary log likelihood to construct individual or cluster
     meat and observed-information bread.

The largest numerical-reference discrepancy was approximately 1.031e-8 under
the recorded normalization, below the declared 3e-7 derivative-reference
tolerance. No production estimator or tolerance was altered in response to
these checks. The script, saved baseline aggregation source, session information,
source hashes, and complete per-check results permit reproduction.

Remaining limits
----------------
Every declared category must have positive frequency, and ordinary observed
bread must be numerically invertible. Nonconverged fits, unresolved curvature,
invalid source labels, unsupported corrections, and fewer than two effective
clusters fail explicitly. Source profile inference and refit bootstrap methods
remain unsupported. The ordinary-score sandwich at a meanBR estimate has the
existing first-order interpretation; it does not differentiate the global
adjustment or establish finite-sample calibration.

Decision
--------
The streamed calculation can use the same public covariance interface and
interpretation as the retained-data calculation. No separate streamed estimator
or replacement bread is needed. Subsequent work can focus on source performance
and broader boundary diagnostics without revising these covariance equations.
