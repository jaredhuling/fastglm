Ordinal fitting from repeatable sources: development validation
==============================================================

Baseline source: c3b3eeb Correct binomial Firth tail calculations and accelerate
ordinal fitting. The completed source hashes, installed library hashes, and
session records identify the implementation checked here. This is engineering
validation of equivalent equations and bounded design storage, not a formal
inferential-calibration study or a claim that source fitting is faster.

Mathematical and computational contract
--------------------------------------
The model is G^{-1}{Pr(Y<=j)}=alpha_j-x'beta-offset. Cumulative logit, probit,
and complementary log-log links, grouped counts, frequency weights, and
mean bias reduction have the same definitions as dense fastglm_ordinal().
Frequency counts are not normalized in the likelihood or estimating equations.

A shared native header now separates accumulation of ordinary scores and
expected information from accumulation of the mean-bias adjustment. The
information is combined across the complete source and inverted before any
row's adjustment is calculated. This is the same global-information adjustment
used by the dense implementation, evaluated during a subsequent source pass.
A further pass evaluates the largest remaining cumulative-predictor change.
The dense evaluator and optimizer use the same kernels and preserve their
previous operation ordering. No probability floor or inferential correction
is added by the source implementation.

Category totals and the predictor schema are established globally. Empty
interior categories have the same reduced-model mapping as dense fits; empty
endpoints require mean bias reduction. Zero-frequency blocks are allowed.
Weighted centering/scaling and an incremental QR rank check combine all blocks;
a single block may be deficient in rank or omit categories. The starting
thresholds and final parameter/covariance transformations match dense fitting.
The declared data source must return unchanged rows and values on every pass.
Structural checks cannot establish immutability of an external database.

With B rows in the largest supplied block, p predictors, K categories, and
C source blocks, additional storage is O(B*(p+K)+(p+K)^2+C), plus the stored
optimizer trace and the source's own metadata. The source callback must itself
read data in blocks; a closure over a full in-memory matrix does not provide
bounded total memory. Predictor matrices, responses, and row-frequency vectors
are not retained in the fitted model. Prediction and residual callbacks keep
output storage bounded; collecting the complete result necessarily uses O(n)
or O(nK) output memory.

Validation scope
----------------
The test suite compares all three links under ML and mean bias reduction,
several source and inner-block sizes, grouped counts, individual outcomes,
frequency weights, zero weights, offsets, and formula sources. It checks
coefficient, covariance, ordinary/adjusted score, likelihood, deviance,
prediction, standard-error, residual, and global-category mapping agreement.
Resettable sources check that each pass begins at block one. Tests reject
changing column/response schemas, redundant global designs, unsupported
noninteger frequencies, and missing numeric category specifications.

run.R fixes seed 114101 before any source comparison. It writes data to
uncompressed temporary RDS files one block at a time (B=2000,p=6,K=4), then
fits ML and mean bias reduction at n=5000 and n=200000. Dense and source
runs execute in separate R processes. The source retains filenames only.
On macOS, /usr/bin/time -l reports maximum process RSS, including R startup,
package loading, data generation and both fits. This is not an isolated native
allocation measurement. R object.size excludes closure environments, so the
reported object size alone cannot establish the memory claim; the allocation
structure and process RSS provide separate evidence. A single timing per case
illustrates rereading costs and is not a precision speed comparison.

Observed results and limits
---------------------------
The initial isolated comparisons converged in seven iterations for both
estimators and implementations. At n=5000, dense/source elapsed seconds were
0.005/0.034 for both ML and meanBR. At n=200000, they were 0.169/1.025 for ML
and 0.219/1.374 for meanBR. The source used 23 and 30 complete passes,
respectively, including preparation. Repeated reads and block validation have
a material cost: this feature offers bounded design storage, not a speedup
over an already available dense matrix. These are single development timings.

Across these comparisons, maximum absolute coefficient, covariance, and score
differences were 2.23e-16, 6.51e-19, and 1.34e-11. Likelihood and deviance
differences were at most 1.82e-12 and 3.64e-12. Predicted probabilities and
their standard errors differed by at most 1.12e-16 and 6.94e-18. All raw
solutions and derived errors are retained in results/.

The initial sandboxed processes could not obtain macOS memory statistics
(the raw logs retain the sysctl failure). Separate fresh processes repeated
the same comparisons with access to those statistics. A one-million-row
comparison then tested whether the source's live storage remained bounded.
Other verification jobs could run concurrently with these memory reruns;
their elapsed times are not used for speed claims. Both macOS process
statistics are reported here, rather than selecting the more favorable one:

                 maximum resident bytes       peak memory footprint bytes
rows             dense         source          dense         source
5,000            226066432     296255488         188072728     226263856
200,000          472219648     584056832         311018456     248923000
1,000,000       1040564224     657686528         647349472     246678392

Source fitting used more maximum resident memory at the two smaller sizes.
At one million rows it used less by both metrics. Its peak footprint was
approximately 247-249 MB at 200000 and one million rows, while its maximum
resident size still increased. Thus the observed operating-system high-water
mark is not identical to bounded live design storage. R allocation, garbage
collection, and operating-system accounting affect these measurements.

At one million rows the source's post-collection live R heap was 107.9 MB
after ML and 108.7 MB after meanBR, with a maximum vector heap of 64 MiB.
For dense fitting the corresponding live-heap measurements were 180.3 MB
and 181.1 MB, and maximum vector heaps were 448.9 and 456.7 MiB. The raw
one-million-row heap CSVs inadvertently label maximum cell counts as
maximum_used_mb; their original numbers are preserved. summarize.R derives
the vector-heap values from the documented 8-byte Vcell unit and does not
infer a byte size for Ncells. Future run.R records both units explicitly.
The live-before/live-after MB fields in the raw records are already correct.

The million-row fits all converged in six iterations. Their maximum
coefficient/covariance/score differences were 1.12e-16, 8.48e-21, and
7.93e-11; likelihood and deviance agreed exactly. Prediction errors were
at most 1.12e-16 and standard-error errors at most 1.52e-18. These checks
support the shared-equations and bounded-design-storage implementation.
They do not establish inferential calibration, precision timing rankings,
or an unconditional bound on memory consumed by a user-supplied callback.

The 5k/200k runs preceded the final R-only removal of repeated category-total
and saturated-likelihood calculations after preparation. Their native-library
hashes are preserved; the historical installed-R database hash was not
recorded and is unavailable. The million-row runs use the final R reader,
whose source and installed-R hashes are in peak-memory/1000000-source-provenance.csv.
The original harness is preserved as results/run-initial.R. The final
source hashes and patch identify the package implementation used for focused
tests; results/source-tests.log records that passing suite. Structural source
validation and all numerical equations were unchanged by the R allocation
reduction. The two stages remain separately identified in the raw results.

Source-fit methods include coefficients, model-based covariance, summaries,
Wald intervals, stored numerical diagnostics, likelihood/deviance, and
predictions/residuals/weights with optional per-block output callbacks. Profile
inference, robust covariance, refitting bootstraps, and methods requiring the
retained fitted observations are not implemented for source fits. Explicit
prediction/evaluation data remain usable by compatible inherited methods.

Reproduce from the repository root with a separately installed candidate:
  /usr/bin/time -l Rscript benchmarks/ordinal-streaming/run.R LIBRARY 5000 dense benchmarks/ordinal-streaming/results/5000-dense > benchmarks/ordinal-streaming/results/5000-dense.log 2>&1
Repeat for n=5000,200000 and method=dense,source, then run summarize.R.
Raw outputs are not overwritten. Use a new results destination for a new
candidate or execution; preserve earlier results and their source provenance.
