Ordinal estimation: development screen
Run: development-v1
This study diagnoses sampling behavior and numerical boundaries; it is not formal confirmation and was not used to tune an estimator or an interval.
Each scenario uses one fixed training design and one fixed held-out design, with independent fresh response seeds. ML and mean bias reduction use the same responses.
Noncollapsed usable success (primary_success) requires convergence, finite slope covariance and no category collapse. Regular-inference success additionally requires a regular finite generating model. These measures are distinct from numerical convergence.
All attempts remain in the denominators. Collapsed-category fits are reported as reduced-model successes when usable, not as numerical failures; they do not count as full-scale regular-inference successes.
Conditional coverage uses primary successes only; joint coverage counts an unsuccessful attempt as not covering. Usable/reduced-model summaries are separate. Nonregular scenarios have no regular full-model coverage interpretation. The 95% Wald intervals use an estimate plus or minus 1.96 model-based standard errors.
The separation flag checks the generating direction only. It is sufficient when all nonempty cumulative splits separate, but is not a general separation detector.
Completed 4800 attempts across 12 scenarios and two estimators.
Runtime in this statistical panel is descriptive and may include concurrent lightweight development work; standalone profile runs are separate.
See summary.csv for failure/probability metrics, slope-summary.csv for bias/RMSE/coverage and Monte Carlo errors, and paired-summary.csv for paired MSE differences with their common-success denominators.
ordinary_small, first slope: ML/meanBR bias 0.0501/0.0012 and RMSE 0.3156/0.2820. The paired squared-error difference (meanBR minus ML) is -0.0201, Monte Carlo SE 0.0031, using 199 common noncollapsed successes.
strong_signal, first slope: ML/meanBR bias 0.3762/0.0193 and RMSE 1.0641/0.8133. The paired squared-error difference (meanBR minus ML) is -0.4709, Monte Carlo SE 0.1158, using 200 common noncollapsed successes.
correlated, first slope: ML/meanBR bias -0.0047/-0.0279 and RMSE 0.5239/0.5038. The paired squared-error difference (meanBR minus ML) is -0.0206, Monte Carlo SE 0.0028, using 200 common noncollapsed successes.
The rare-interior scenario collapsed a category in 70.0% of datasets. The fit still converged in 200/200 meanBR attempts; reduced-model and noncollapsed-model inference are kept separate.
The finite rare-endpoint scenario had empty endpoints in 0.0% of these datasets. The separate structural-empty-endpoint case supplies a deliberate boundary test; it is not evidence about a regular finite-threshold sampling distribution.
Across regular scenarios with at least 100 noncollapsed successes, ML marginal Wald coverage ranges from 0.910 to 0.970; associated Monte Carlo standard errors range from 0.012 to 0.020. These selected ranges are not simultaneous coverage guarantees.
Across regular scenarios with at least 100 noncollapsed successes, meanBR marginal Wald coverage ranges from 0.920 to 0.970; associated Monte Carlo standard errors range from 0.012 to 0.019. These selected ranges are not simultaneous coverage guarantees.
The structural-zero and deterministic-separation scenarios are nonregular. Their finite-fit, probability, and explicitly defined common-slope summaries do not support regular full-model interval claims.
No interval correction or estimator setting was selected from these coverage results. The observed shortfalls motivate targeted inference validation, especially under correlated predictors, rather than a numerical adjustment to achieve 95%.
Pinned external checks: 8/8 comparisons with MASS 7.3.65 ML and WeightIt 2.1.0 meanBR pass. Maximum coefficient/probability discrepancies are 4.43e-07/1.25e-07; grouped and frequency-expanded coefficients differ by at most 1.55e-15.
Standalone profiles use 20,000 rows with 3 predictors and 4 categories, or 12 predictors and 8 categories. Timings describe this machine and are separate from statistical-panel elapsed times.

The native fitter accounts for 50.0-77.0% of sampled fit-plus-prediction time; probability prediction accounts for 16.1-32.0%.
R-managed allocation concentrations occur in initialization rank-QR/centering copies and prediction's cumulative-probability outer products and block intermediates. These are concrete candidates for allocation reduction; no package changes were made in this study.
Rprof samples R call stacks and does not identify individual C++ instructions. Rprofmem includes R-managed allocations above 64 KiB, excluding Eigen/C++ allocations. R vector-heap maxima include retained designs and benchmark fits; they are not incremental fit memory, process RSS or native peak memory.
