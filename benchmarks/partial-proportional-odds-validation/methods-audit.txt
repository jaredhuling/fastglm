Partial proportional odds: adjustment, assumptions, and independent validation
2026-11-04

Conclusion and scope
--------------------
The dense adjustment implements the expected-information mean-bias-reducing
equations for the specified cumulative-link model in its original coefficients.
The independent confirmation passed 185 of 185 prespecified comparisons. This
supports exposing mean bias reduction for identifiable, interior dense models,
provided the fitter retains explicit probability, conditioning, and convergence
checks. It does not establish universal finite estimates, a unique root, global
convergence, or accurate finite-sample Wald coverage. Empty-category boundary
mapping and special likelihood/score inference need separate work.

Model and sampling assumptions
------------------------------
For q = K - 1, let eta_ij = alpha_j - x_i' beta - z_i' gamma_j - offset_i,
P_ik = G(eta_ik) - G(eta_i,k-1), with endpoint cumulative probabilities 0 and 1.
The common and nonparallel design columns are disjoint. Parameters are stored
as alpha, beta, and threshold-major gamma blocks; there is no extra intercept.
The logit link gives partial proportional odds. The same design with probit or
complementary log-log is a nonparallel cumulative-link model.

The likelihood conditions on the design and offsets. Independent categorical
outcomes, or independent multinomial groups with fixed totals, supply its
sampling interpretation. Integer frequency weights reproduce independent
observations; multiplying a whole row's log likelihood is not a cluster model.
Noninteger weights extend the estimating equations algebraically but are not
literal replications and are not justified as survey or inverse-probability
weights by this audit. The published wine calculation is reproduced under its
stated likelihood, without making claims about independence of repeated ratings.

For bias and asymptotic covariance claims, additional regularity is required:
fixed finite parameter dimension; an identifiable true parameter in the interior
of the probability domain; sufficiently smooth probabilities and differentiation
under expectation; nonsingular information increasing at the regular sample
size rate; and a consistent local root. Uniform integrability and higher-order
conditions needed by a bias expansion are not supplied by a convergence flag.
These conditions concern sequences of models and samples, not just this finite
test suite. Covariance I(theta)^(-1) is first-order model-based covariance.

Independent derivation from joint categorical contributions
----------------------------------------------------------
This calculation starts from the general expected-cumulant expression and does
not assume the cumulative adjusted-count formula. For a single categorical draw
in row i, let u_ik = grad log P_ik and H_ik = Hess log P_ik when category k occurs.
Write m_i for the effective number of draws, and I for total expected information.
Independence and zero expected scores eliminate cross-observation contributions:

 I = sum_i m_i sum_k P_ik u_ik u_ik'
 P_t + Q_t = sum_i m_i sum_k P_ik (u_ik u_ik' + H_ik) u_ik,t
 A_t = (1/2) trace{I^(-1) (P_t + Q_t)}.

Here Q_t = E[H U_t], equivalently minus the expected observed-information/score
product; using the opposite sign would give the wrong adjustment. Since

 Hess P_ik = P_ik (H_ik + u_ik u_ik'),

the expression reduces exactly to

 A_t = (1/2) sum_i m_i sum_k (grad P_ik)_t / P_ik
                                      * trace{I^(-1) Hess P_ik}.

Let a_ij be row j of the linear predictor design. Its Hessian is zero and

 Hess G(eta_ij) = G''(eta_ij) a_ij a_ij'.

Defining c_ij = (m_i/2) G''(eta_ij) a_ij' I^(-1) a_ij, with c_i0=c_iK=0,
therefore gives A = sum_ik grad P_ik (c_ik-c_i,k-1)/P_ik. This derives the
native expression from the full joint categorical likelihood. It applies to
any identified linear cumulative predictor, including the proposed partial
design, and does not replace a categorical outcome by independent binaries.

The numerical reference computes u and H with numDeriv from log probabilities
and constructs P_t+Q_t directly. It never uses G'', leverage diagonals, or the
adjusted cumulative counts. It also verifies I against the expected negative
log-probability Hessian. Thus the reference is independent of the native
adjustment formula, although both necessarily describe the same model.

Linear coordinates, replication, and domains
-------------------------------------------
For an invertible constant map theta = S delta, category scores and Hessians
transform as u_delta=S'u_theta and H_delta=S'H_theta S. Substitution in the
trace expression gives A_delta=S'A_theta and I_delta=S'I_theta S. The adjusted
root and its covariance transform accordingly. Centering/scaling predictors
and baseline-plus-deviation slope coordinates are covered by this result.
Nonlinear maps introduce Hessians of the map; reducing bias after a nonlinear
log-gap reparameterization generally targets a different estimator.

Grouping independent identical design points adds their information, scores,
and cumulant contributions, so grouped and expanded frequency data agree.
Scaling all frequencies by c scales I, U, and P_t+Q_t by c, leaving A unchanged.
Offsets enter the probabilities but have no coefficient derivatives. Zero
frequency contributes neither likelihood nor adjustment and imposes no training
domain constraint, although prediction at that row must still be validated.

For a positive-frequency row, each adjacent gap is affine in z:
 alpha_(j+1)-alpha_j - z'(gamma_(j+1)-gamma_j) > 0.
Consequently training feasibility extends to the convex hull of its expanded
nonparallel design. Ordering alpha alone is insufficient; conversely alpha may
be unordered if the design origin is outside that domain. Unless consecutive
slopes coincide, the gap fails in some unbounded direction. Extrapolated
probabilities require checks and cannot be repaired by clipping or reordering.

Numerical results and limits
----------------------------
The frozen protocol precedes the native confirmation. Results are under
results-2026-11-04/, separate from the earlier exploratory prototype.
Across two fresh fixed fixtures and three links, maximum absolute differences:
 ordinary score versus numerical derivatives: 1.18e-8;
 generic cumulant adjustment versus native adjustment: 1.73e-9;
 baseline-plus-deviation adjustment transformation: 2.04e-10;
 native predictor-recoding identities: 1.43e-14.
All common-slope, binary, grouped expansion, frequency scaling, offsets, and
zero-frequency domain checks pass. The twelve fresh fitted roots (two designs,
three links, ML and mean bias reduction) agree with the original external
bpolr implementation within 3.91e-11 for coefficients and 2.46e-12 for covariance.
Both solvers converged in every case. These are deterministic equation/root
checks, not a study of bias, rejection probabilities, or coverage.

The wine mean-bias-reduced fit agrees with the original author implementation
within 7.76e-12 for coefficients and 1.63e-11 for covariance. Its standardized
adjusted score is 5.10e-12 and remaining predictor update 8.39e-12. The original
implementation gives alpha_2 = 1.054511572048214. Its archived Section1_11.R
output prints 1.055 at three decimals; the paper prints 1.06 at two decimals.
This is consistent with double rounding during presentation. Direct rounding
to two decimals gives 1.05. There is no evidence of a different statistical
target, and no coefficient or comparison tolerance was tuned to the table.

The native solver and bpolr are distinct implementations but use the same
established adjustment, which is why the independent generic calculation is
also necessary. Moderate interior fixtures do not validate extreme-tail
arithmetic or every difficult design. Production unit tests separately cover
probability boundaries, rejected extrapolations, and solver failure handling.

Reproduction
------------
Install the candidate fastglm into a separate library. Cache the cited PDF and
supplement outside the package, extracting supplementary/bpolr.R unchanged.
The external implementation needs ordinal, Formula, and gnm; numDeriv supplies
numerical derivatives. No package-runtime dependency is added. Run from the
repository root, always with a fresh output directory:

 Rscript benchmarks/partial-proportional-odds-validation/confirm.R \
   FASTGLM_LIBRARY NEW_OUTPUT_DIRECTORY REFERENCE_CACHE REFERENCE_LIBRARY

The original run used /private/tmp/fastglm-ppo-lib and reference cache/library
under /private/tmp/fastglm-ordinal-reference-cache and
/private/tmp/fastglm-ordinal-reference-lib. Session details, source hashes,
all comparisons, and fitted objects are retained with the results. The source
baseline was commit 45c0ff0 plus the new native partial-model implementation.
The retained results document equations and numerical roots only; their hashes
identify the exact implementation tested if subsequent code changes occur.

Final arithmetic regression confirmation, 2026-11-04
---------------------------------------------------
After the initial confirmation, the native implementation was revised to
accumulate cumulative predictors and adjacent gaps using compensated sums of
their original coefficient terms. This prevents large opposing contributions
from erasing thresholds, offsets, or coefficient-difference bits before they
cancel. The adjustment and fitting equations are unchanged. The final header
SHA256 is 9f3014ef7953e236513a3cfc508ad86242a3fb34becc33c5a85b59d172be7aa6.

The complete protocol was repeated in results-final, with 185/185 comparisons
passing. This repeat uses the same fixtures and external examples and is a
regression confirmation, not additional independent samples. Its source hashes
and fits identify the final tested implementation. Separate package tests cover
the arithmetic counterexamples and retain an extreme complementary-log-log
case whose information is too poorly conditioned for reliable covariance.
