Noncanonical binomial Firth iteration: development and confirmation
=================================================================

Baseline: 3f4a407c3fbbcf483fee4680557ac1f21aec64c3 (fastglm 0.1.3).
Candidate: baseline plus implementation.patch. protocol-sha256.txt records the
patch and run.R hashes frozen before the confirmation outputs were inspected.
Session information and installed native-library hashes accompany the results.
This is an engineering validation of a numerical iteration, not a study of
coverage, false-positive rates, or a new bias-reduction estimator.

Mechanism and development decisions
-----------------------------------
The grouped housing cloglog example lost its working information after an
oversized scoring proposal. The dense baseline QR fit returned rank zero and
nonconvergence after six iterations; LLT rejected singular information. A
coherent zero-coefficient start let both fastglm and brglm2 reach the same
finite solution. The default brglm2 start also failed on this example.

initial-prototype.R reproduces an R-level investigation of the adjusted score.
Limiting the largest positive-weight predictor update to one link-scale unit
recovered the finite root. A score-norm halving prototype had an inconsistent
initial state and accepted its last trial after exhausting the search. It was
rejected, and is retained as exploration rather than a correct solver.

The first native candidate bounded updates but retained delayed information in
sparse and mapped fits. The deliberately all-zero weighted housing example
revealed persistent oscillation: cloglog remained around a standardized adjusted
score of 0.18 after 200 iterations. The recorded lagged-adjustment.log preserves
that failed candidate's behavior. Using current information for the adjustment
removed this delay. Streamed all-zero/all-one fits with offsets also attempted
an infinite ML null intercept; their null deviance is now evaluated as zero
directly. This is a reporting calculation, separate from the adjusted fit.

Operating rule
--------------
For binomial probit and cloglog Firth fits, form the existing adjusted scoring
proposal. Multiply its direction by min(1, 1 / max_i |x_i' delta|), with the
maximum over positive-weight observations. Nonfinite proposals are failures;
a capped proposal cannot alone trigger coefficient-change convergence. Sparse,
mapped, and source paths use current information for the adjustment with these
links. Existing stopping checks and adjusted-score diagnostics remain in place.

The multiplier is strictly positive for a finite, nonzero proposal and equals
one near a regular root. It therefore preserves adjusted-score fixed points
in exact arithmetic. Predictor-based limiting is invariant to invertible column
recoding in exact arithmetic. Neither property is a proof of global or local
convergence. Numerical derivative floors, conditioning, and stopping tolerances
still matter. The rule does not change standard errors or calibrate inference.

Frozen numerical confirmation
-----------------------------
run.R uses an untouched seed (111901): six ordinary family/link fits with 5,000
rows and six coefficients, plus probit/cloglog separation, all-zero, all-one,
and grouped responses with offsets and zero/integer weights. The latter use
240 rows, three coefficients, and dense, sparse, mapped, streamed QR, and
streamed LLT representations. Both baseline and candidate use tol=1e-9 and
maxit=300. Independent brglm2 AS_mean comparisons use explicit zero starts,
epsilon=1e-10 and maxit=500. Errors and warnings are retained, not discarded.

Successful fits increased from 30/46 to 41/46, retaining every baseline success.
All 41 candidate successes matched converged reference coefficients within
6.4e-9. For separated probit data, sparse/mapped iterations fell from 260 to 70;
streamed LLT fell from 259 to 68, and streamed QR converged in 69 rather than
reaching its limit. For all-one probit data, sparse/mapped iterations fell from
144 to 42. Housing regression tests separately cover both links, QR/LLT/LDLT,
grouping, offsets, zero weights, mapped input, multiple block sizes, recoding,
and rejection of false convergence after a capped tiny coefficient update.

The remaining five failures are ONE separated cloglog dataset across all five
representations. The reference also failed with its default scoring factor.
Post-confirmation development, explore-separation.R, found that stronger fixed
reference damping (slowit=0.1) reaches a finite solution in 285 iterations;
slowit=0.5 still failed at 1,000. The ordinal solver also failed on the equivalent
binary problem. This indicates an unresolved iteration problem, not evidence
that the adjusted estimate must be infinite. Starting fastglm at the damped
reference solution gave a one-step change of 9.5e-10 and a standardized adjusted
score of 2.1e-10, confirming agreement at that root. The patch is not a universal cure
for separated noncanonical models. Further work should assess a score-based
step acceptance rule or locally adapted scoring direction; no such rule is
selected from these results, and no new inferential claim follows from them.

Timing tradeoffs
----------------
The small stress timings in results/*.csv use system.time and have millisecond
resolution, so iteration counts and successful completion are more informative
than precise speed ratios there. Source fits can need additional passes for
current information and for proposals whose predictor bound exceeds one.
They may be slower on easy data or slow storage despite improved robustness.

time-ordinary.R is a separate fresh-seed (112001) timing check with 12,000 rows,
six coefficients, warmup and 41 microbenchmark repetitions. The two libraries
ran sequentially on the same machine. Median milliseconds (baseline / candidate):
logit 5.586 / 5.415; probit 6.774 / 7.624; cloglog 8.917 / 9.357;
Poisson log 6.109 / 6.054; Gamma log 4.402 / 4.395; Gaussian 1.533 / 1.636.
Iteration counts were unchanged in this timing panel. In particular, ordinary
probit and cloglog paid approximately 13% and 5% overhead in this run. The
unaffected paths vary in both directions; these are single-machine timings,
not formal equivalence tests or guarantees about another workload.

Reproduction
------------
Install the pinned baseline in one isolated R library and the patched source
in another, using a clean native rebuild for each. From the repository root:

  Rscript benchmarks/firth-noncanonical/run.R BASELINE_LIBRARY baseline NEW_OUTPUT
  Rscript benchmarks/firth-noncanonical/run.R CANDIDATE_LIBRARY safeguarded NEW_OUTPUT
  Rscript benchmarks/firth-noncanonical/time-ordinary.R BASELINE_LIBRARY baseline NEW_OUTPUT
  Rscript benchmarks/firth-noncanonical/time-ordinary.R CANDIDATE_LIBRARY safeguarded NEW_OUTPUT
  Rscript benchmarks/firth-noncanonical/development/initial-prototype.R BASELINE_LIBRARY
  Rscript benchmarks/firth-noncanonical/development/explore-separation.R CANDIDATE_LIBRARY

Each runner refuses to replace its output CSV. Run timing processes sequentially
without other fitting or compilation jobs. The frozen patch concerns fitting;
the accompanying Wald interface and documentation changes do not affect this
comparison. These tests cover a finite panel and do not establish global
convergence, high-dimensional performance, or small-sample inferential accuracy.
