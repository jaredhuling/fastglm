Dense partial ordinal fitting: bounded performance results
2026-11-04

All twelve fastglm partial-model cases completed their warm-up and three measured
fits with valid convergence, covariance, and probabilities. Four of six ML
comparisons with ordinal::clm met both packages' convergence criteria; their
clm/fastglm median-time ratios were 1.19 to 2.62. The two unsuccessful comparator
warm-ups are retained and excluded from ratios. No runtime-budget reductions
were needed.

Timings include the public formula interface, fitting, and covariance extraction.
ML and mean bias reduction used the same data within each size/link combination.
MeanBR has no timed external comparator here. The partial model has four ordered
categories, two common predictors, and one predictor whose slope differs across
the three thresholds. Its eight coefficients have a dense joint covariance.

* At n = 100000, clm reached its iteration limit for probit and complementary
  log-log under the frozen gradTol=1e-7 setting. Absolute maximum gradients were
  2.47e-7 and 8.43e-6, respectively. Both covariance matrices were positive
  definite, and their probabilities agreed with fastglm within 5.97e-12 and
  2.17e-10; log-likelihood differences were below 1.87e-9. This is consistent
  with difficulty meeting a stringent absolute gradient criterion near the
  numerical optimum, rather than a materially different fitted model. Their
  approximately ten-second unsuccessful warm-ups are not speed-comparison
  evidence. The stopping rule was not relaxed after inspecting these results.

For the four successful comparator pairs, all-row probability differences were
below 3.01e-11 and log-likelihood differences below 1.46e-10. The fastglm partial
and common-slope fits all passed the prespecified checks: maximum standardized
score 8.64e-9, remaining predictor update 1.54e-10, normalization error 4.45e-16,
and minimum information reciprocal condition number 0.110. Each fit's actual
diagnostics, warnings and status appear in results-2026-11-04/all-fits.csv.

Common-slope regression check
----------------------------
For n = 100000, four categories and three common predictors, candidate and
preserved-baseline outputs agreed exactly in returned coefficients, covariance,
log likelihood, and probabilities at the fixed comparison rows. Input hashes
also matched exactly. Both libraries completed all warm-ups and three repeats.

                               ML                     Mean bias reduction
Link                    baseline candidate          baseline candidate
logit                         57        57                90        85
probit                       131       130               245       234
complementary log-log        164       165               296       319

Times are milliseconds. ML medians differed by less than 1%; meanBR candidate/
baseline ratios ranged from 0.944 to 1.078. Three desktop repeats cannot resolve
small timing changes reliably, especially with overlapping observed ranges.
These results do not establish a speed improvement or regression in the
unchanged common-slope path.

Environment and interpretation
------------------------------
The run used R 4.5.1 on aarch64 macOS Sequoia 15.7.3, base R BLAS/LAPACK, and
ordinal 2023.12-4.1. Numerical-library thread counts were requested as one.
Workers ran serially, with no concurrent package compilation, tests, rendering,
or other package work. Ordinary desktop background load remained: one unrelated
process was using approximately one CPU core. These are descriptive timings
from this machine and workload, not a universal performance claim.

The retained record contains 114 fits: 96 valid fastglm fits, 16 valid clm fits,
and two clm warm-ups with convergence warnings. All three worker processes
exited successfully. Output was written to a fresh directory; the protocol and
timing scripts remained frozen. No inference simulation or calibration was
performed, and no package code changed in response to the timing results.
