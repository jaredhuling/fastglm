Logistic ordinal probability and derivative calculations
=======================================================

Baseline: e94b066737740d548791c391493277fad925e59c (fastglm 0.1.3).
The source patch and added source files are recorded in the adjacent
binomial-tail-accuracy study. Installed native-library hashes and session
information identify the binaries actually measured here. The later binomial
state-cache/direct-product changes do not change the ordinal evaluator or its
logistic R interface. The subsequent R-side cloglog tail correction concerns
a different link.

Motivation and derivation
------------------------
The previous ordinal-fit-profile study found the native evaluator dominated
large-data fitting. For cumulative logistic probabilities F_j and density g_j,
write P_j = F_j-F_(j-1), d_j = alpha_j-alpha_(j-1), and S_j=1-F_j.
For an interior category,
  log(P_j) = log(F_j) + log(S_(j-1)) + log(1-exp(-d_j)),
  g_j/P_j = S_j + 1/expm1(d_j),
  g_j/P_(j+1) = F_j + 1/expm1(d_(j+1)).
The adjacent-category probability sum is
  P_j+P_(j+1) = F_(j+1)*S_(j-1)*(1-exp(-(d_j+d_(j+1)))).
Endpoints use the corresponding cumulative probability or survival function.
These are algebraic rearrangements of the same likelihood, information, and
mean-bias adjustment. Threshold-only quantities are calculated once per
evaluation. The three extra vectors have length K-1, independent of n.
No observation-level probability array is retained.

Validation and timing
---------------------
Existing numerical derivative tests check likelihood scores, expected
information, and bias adjustments. test-ordinal-logistic-kernel.R additionally
checks direct exponential formulas, narrow category gaps, extreme thresholds,
zero frequencies, and different row blocks. This is implementation validation,
not a new estimator or a study of inferential calibration.

run.R uses a separate seed (112301) and three designs: (n,p,K)=(10000,3,8),
(100000,6,5), and (1000000,3,4). Both ML and mean bias reduction are evaluated,
with ordinary and prepared interfaces. One warm-up precedes three elapsed-time
measurements per case. Baseline and candidate run in separate R processes,
without competing fitting or compilation jobs. Baseline runs first, so thermal
or time-order effects are not eliminated. Median times have millisecond
resolution; the smallest cases should not be read as precise universal ratios.

Across the 12 comparisons, median speedups were 1.73 to 2.73. At one million
observations, ordinary ML fitting fell from 1.019 to 0.512 seconds; ordinary
mean bias reduction fell from 1.916 to 0.954 seconds. All iteration counts
were identical. Maximum coefficient and predicted-probability differences
were 2.23e-16 and 1.67e-16; maximum covariance difference was 1.31e-18.
The maximum absolute log-likelihood difference was 2.33e-10 at n=1,000,000.
Individual results and numerical comparisons accompany the scripts.

These gains apply to the logistic ordinal evaluator. They do not establish
similar gains for other cumulative links, predictor dimensions, category
patterns, or external packages. No cross-package timing claim is made.

Reproduction: install the baseline commit and candidate into separate libraries,
then run run.R LIBRARY LABEL (baseline or candidate) from the repository root.
Use a new results directory or preserve existing results before a new run;
run.R refuses to replace recorded raw results. Run summarize.R for summaries.
