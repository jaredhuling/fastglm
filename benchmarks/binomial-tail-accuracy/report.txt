Accurate noncanonical binomial adjusted scores
=============================================

Baseline: e94b066737740d548791c391493277fad925e59c (fastglm 0.1.3).
The source patch records tracked changes; source-additions contains added
implementation files. Session records and native-library hashes identify the
installed builds. The manifest records the completed artifacts. Earlier
firth-noncanonical results are preserved without alteration.

Scope and equations
-------------------
This work corrects numerical evaluation of established mean bias-reduction
equations for binomial probit and complementary log-log models. It is not a
new estimator or an inferential calibration method. No coverage or rejection
rate study was used to choose the calculation or convergence criteria.
The relevant score, expected-information, and AS_mean adjustment equations were
read in the brglm2 iteration vignette, sections "Generalized linear models",
"Bias-reducing adjusted score functions", and "Iteration":
https://ikosmidis.r-universe.dev/brglm2/doc/iteration.html
No universal finiteness or global-convergence result is claimed here.

For response proportion y, effective trials m, design row x, linear predictor
eta, mean p, derivative d, and second derivative d2, the row quantities are
  ordinary score = m*d*(y-p)/(p*(1-p)),
  working information weight = m*d^2/(p*(1-p)),
  adjustment = h*d2/(2*d), where h=w*x'*(X'WX)^(-1)*x.
For cloglog, t=exp(eta), log(1-p)=-t, and
  w=m*exp(2*eta-t-log(p)),
  score=m*(y*exp(eta-t-log(p))-(1-y)*t), d2/d=1-t.
For probit, logarithms of both normal tails and the normal density give the
same quantities without subtracting a rounded mean from one; d2/d=-eta.
Zero counts and zero weights are handled before potentially infinite products.
In ranges where direct products are safe, the implementation reuses the
probabilities and derivative directly; these are the same algebraic formulas.
Dense, mapped, and sparse fits cache the current state's score, information,
and deviance quantities. A predictor change invalidates this cache.

Findings from development
-------------------------
The earlier safeguard study left one separated cloglog example unresolved.
Strong damping let brglm2 report convergence near (-0.6380,14.2126,1.4891).
However, probability and derivative floors change the adjusted equations in
that example. Independent tail calculations give adjusted score approximately
(0.06778,0.13240,0.01110) there, with a remaining predictor change about 5.47.
Thus the earlier interpretation of that reference fit as the desired finite
solution was incomplete. diagnose.R reproduces this comparison.

Accurate tail calculations instead give a root near
(-0.90585,30.42328,4.74168). The native GLM and independently implemented binary
ordinal evaluator agree after accounting for the sign convention. The
ordinal binary solver permits nonmonotone adjusted-score norms: the Fisher
scoring direction is not a gradient direction for that norm, and a monotone
norm search can stall at a positive-score point. It retains finite-domain
checks, bounded predictor changes, and final score/predictor convergence checks.

An early R prototype formed leverages from squared rows of Q. Absolute
roundoff in a nearly zero row of Q can produce a spurious adjustment when
combined with enormous cloglog curvature or divided by a tiny working weight.
The corrected independent calculation uses w*x'*(X'WX)^(-1)*x. Development
logs retain the earlier misleading calculations and the subsequent correction;
they are not all accepted validation evidence. A separate probit example
(seed 112101, also tested at 112102) exposed the same issue in dense native QR.
The corrected QR and SVD implementations form row quadratic quantities before
applying small weights. This avoids amplifying Q/U roundoff.

The returned fitted probabilities retain the ordinary family convention and
can round to zero or one. Likelihood, weights, adjusted and ordinary scores,
covariance, residuals, and probability derivative calculations use eta directly
for fits carrying the new internal marker. Older saved fits are not relabeled
as having solved the new numerical equations. Extremely bad starting values
can still underflow working information or fail; such cases are not evidence
of a solved system.

Numerical confirmation
----------------------
run.R preserves the RNG sequence of the previous 111901 panel for the two
noncanonical binomial links. It adds a separate 112201 panel at n=120 and 300,
with separated, grouped, all-zero, and all-one outcomes; frequency weights,
zero weights, offsets, and nuisance predictors are included. Six computation
paths are tested for each stress case: dense QR/LLT, sparse LLT, mapped LLT,
streamed QR/LLT. These 146 fits use tol=1e-9 and maxit=500. This panel was not
used to tune inferential rejection rates. Development examples and intermediate
candidate results are retained separately from the final implementation.

The baseline reported convergence for 134 of 146 fits. Six of those converged
fits did not solve the accurately evaluated equations (standardized adjusted
score about 0.0483). The remaining 12 failures were separated cloglog cases.
The final numerical results and performance tradeoffs are recorded below and
in results/accuracy-summary.csv and results/timing-comparison.csv.

Tests also compare independent scores and information, binary ordinal models,
all dense decomposition methods, row partitions, grouping/expansion, predictor
recoding, zero weights, offsets, residual identities, likelihood conventions,
prediction derivatives, and missing-row handling. Existing moderate-data
brglm2 comparisons are retained; agreement with a clipped extreme-data root
is deliberately not used as the accuracy criterion.

Performance and limits
----------------------
The ordinary timing protocol is the existing firth-noncanonical/time-ordinary.R
script: n=12000, five predictors plus intercept, six families, 41 timed fits
per family. Baseline runs first; elapsed differences are descriptive and may
include machine/time-order effects. Initial candidate and state-cache-only
timings are retained to show the cost and effect of the subsequent exact
algebraic optimization. Final timings use label "products".

These changes resolve the tested failures and improve equation accuracy.
They do not guarantee convergence for every design or starting value, improve
small-sample Wald calibration, or remove the cost of exact tail calculations.
No bootstrap calibration, mixed-effects extension, or external Windows check
is part of this work.

Reproduction from repository root
---------------------------------
Install baseline and final sources into separate libraries. Run:
  Rscript benchmarks/binomial-tail-accuracy/run.R LIBRARY LABEL
  Rscript benchmarks/firth-noncanonical/time-ordinary.R LIBRARY LABEL benchmarks/binomial-tail-accuracy/results
  Rscript benchmarks/binomial-tail-accuracy/diagnose.R LIBRARY
  Rscript benchmarks/binomial-tail-accuracy/summarize.R LABEL
Use label baseline for the baseline and products for the final candidate.
Raw result files are not overwritten; preserve them and use a fresh destination
before rerunning. The final scripts and source snapshot are hashed in the
manifest; these hashes record the completed state, not a preregistration.

Build sequence
--------------
The candidate and final labels precede the state-cache optimization; final
also includes the aligned streamed Pearson summary and removal of an unused
ordinal helper. The cached timing label records reuse of each binomial state.
The products label is the completed implementation, including exact direct
products in safe ranges and stable R-side ordinal tail logs. No raw result
from an earlier candidate was replaced.

Completed results
-----------------
All 146 final fits converged; the maximum independent standardized adjusted
score was 8.02e-9. Maximum relative covariance discrepancy was 1.88e-14, and
maximum discrepancy between reported and independently evaluated adjusted
scores was 2.35e-13. In the original difficult cloglog example, the native GLM
and binary ordinal coefficients differed by 7.52e-11 at tol=1e-10.

For ordinary n=12000 data, final median probit time was 11.25 ms versus 7.35 ms
for the baseline (1.53 times as long); cloglog was 12.51 versus 8.71 ms
(1.44 times as long). The initial exact-tail implementation took 13.55 and
15.41 ms respectively. Caching and exact direct products reduced that cost,
but did not eliminate the overhead relative to the floored calculation.
Other families' measured ratios were 1.00 to 1.09; their fitting equations
and core iteration were not changed by this optimization. These comparisons
do not establish speed equivalence under every workload.

The complete testthat suite passed. The three process-worker tests also passed
when local sockets were available. Arrow-specific tests were skipped because
Arrow is not installed. Both revised vignettes rendered successfully to HTML
in the vignettes directory. Local build/check details are recorded separately
in results/local-check.txt.
