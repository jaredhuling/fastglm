Generated by `report.R` from the complete stored records. Methods and interval rules were fixed before the main run; no production estimator or uncertainty formula was changed.

The main panel contains 1000 datasets and 2000 original fits. Each converged original fit requested 199 bootstrap refits. All five cells contain 200 paired datasets.

Coverage is reported conditional on an interval being available. `Available and covers` is the probability of obtaining a covering interval, including unavailable results as absence of a covering result. These are distinct quantities. Parentheses contain Monte Carlo standard errors. Finite bootstrap percentile sampling error is additional to the between-dataset uncertainty described by those Monte Carlo standard errors.

## Interpretation

In the regular independent-observation cell, first-slope coverage ranged from 0.950 to 0.965 across the fixed procedures, with Monte Carlo standard errors from 0.013 to 0.015. This panel gives no clear ranking among these procedures in that setting. It does not establish equivalence.

Rare-category bootstrap availability was the main practical limitation: only 7 of 200 ML and 16 of 200 meanBR datasets received percentile intervals. MeanBR retained finite original fits, but finite estimates did not prevent resampled empty categories. Conditional coverage on these small selected subsets cannot support a general rare-category calibration claim. A zero plug-in Monte Carlo standard error after no observed misses also does not imply that coverage is known precisely.

For the clustered cell, first-slope model-based coverage was 0.845 for ML and 0.850 for meanBR. Cluster HC1 coverage was 0.925 and 0.935, respectively; the paired gains over the independence-based comparator were 0.080 (MCSE 0.019) and 0.085 (MCSE 0.020). These differences support accounting for clustering in this design. The finite-sample accuracy of the cluster approximation remains distinct from that improvement.

Whole-cluster percentile coverage for the first slope was 0.900 (MCSE 0.021) for ML and 0.905 (MCSE 0.021) for meanBR. Thus the percentile procedure did not attain nominal coverage in the observed panel, despite all its intervals being available. This is evidence of a finite-sample limitation to investigate, rather than a reason to tune the critical value or discard refits. Two hundred datasets and 199 bootstrap draws limit the precision of that assessment.

The result supports retaining explicit category-support and cluster-approximation limits. It does not justify changing an inference default. The next substantive questions are uncertainty at sparse category boundaries and calibration with varying numbers of independent clusters; probability-model misspecification requires a separate study with a verified pseudo-true target. No additional scenarios or estimator changes were introduced after inspecting this panel.

Bootstrap availability also depends on the fixed number of refits: the chance of encountering at least one empty-category resample increases as more refits are drawn. Reducing the number of refits to avoid that event would not establish valid uncertainty. A target-specific justification that distinguishes retained empty endpoints under meanBR from interior-category collapse is a separate theoretical task.

## Scope of the result

These are marginal 95% intervals for four-category models with two predictors. The full parameter tables, directional misses, conditional widths, paired contrasts with Monte Carlo standard errors, and simultaneous-inclusion diagnostics are in the accompanying CSV files. All-five-parameter inclusion is descriptive because no simultaneous 95% procedure is used.

The independent-cluster cell has a correctly specified marginal proportional-odds model but dependent outcomes. Its expected-Fisher Wald variance ignores that dependence and is included as a diagnostic comparator. HC0/HC1 use cluster sums; the whole-cluster bootstrap resamples independent clusters. Thirty clusters need not suffice for the large-cluster approximation.

This bounded development panel does not establish calibration under probability-model misspecification, with very few clusters, at structural empty-category boundaries, in growing dimension, or across arbitrary predictor distributions. A coverage difference comparable to its Monte Carlo uncertainty is inconclusive. No interval default is changed from these results.

`audit.txt` records complete-grid and failure-policy checks. `manifest.rds`, the archived `runner.R`, `installed-hashes.csv`, `source-hashes.csv`, and `session-info.txt` identify the run. All failures are present in raw records and diagnostics.
