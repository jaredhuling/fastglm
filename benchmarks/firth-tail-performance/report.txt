Binomial Firth finalization and derivative performance
=====================================================

Role and baseline
-----------------
This is an implementation optimization of the accurate-tail equations from
c3b3eeb2d217727e5a4194382bdd5925cc4d2cab. It aims to recover avoidable cost while
retaining that commit's estimator, numerical safeguards, and reported quantities.
It does not change statistical assumptions, inference approximations, tuning,
mean-bias adjustment, or convergence criteria. This study compares accurate
implementations; an older calculation using probability floors is not an
accuracy comparator.

The baseline source was archived from that commit and its matching installed
library was copied into an immutable separate library before editing. The final
source delta is implementation.patch; apply it to the baseline and regenerate
Rcpp exports before installation. protocol-sha256.txt records the candidate,
protocol, runner, tests, and reused reference-script hashes before confirmation.
The installed native-library hashes and session files identify measured builds.
Other ordinal extensions may coexist in the candidate package, but are not
called by this binomial benchmark or changed by this patch.

Development findings
--------------------
Separate profiling at n=12,000 and 100,000 identified post-fit accounting as
roughly a quarter of complete fitting time for the probit/cloglog examples.
The previous R finalization first computed ordinary residuals and then replaced
them with accurate-tail residuals. It materialized seven moment vectors before
constructing additional vectors for likelihoods and residuals. Probability
standard-error requests also computed all seven vectors, plus two dummy input
vectors, although only a derivative was needed.

The first candidate replaces finalization with one native pass returning the
two required row vectors and scalar deviance/AIC totals. Its trial and frequency
conventions remain those of the existing helper: use original trial totals if
any exceed one, otherwise use prior weights; round trial and success counts for
the binomial normalizer. Zero-count products with infinite log probabilities
remain excluded. Working residuals use the stable formulas already implemented,
including tails where the ordinary derivative underflows. A separate derivative
kernel allocates only its requested result. Older saved fits and other families
retain their existing derivative convention.

The first candidate reduced finalization from about 17–19 ms to 5.5–6.0 ms at
n=100,000 in the development inputs. Complete fits improved about 18–19% and
these derivative requests improved about 3.6–5.9 times. These profiles informed
the implementation; final performance claims use the separate confirmation
runner and fresh seed, not selected development timings.

A second exact duplication occurred in native reporting. Dense, sparse, and
mapped return wrappers evaluated ordinary deviance and then stable binomial
deviance although the solver cache already held that stable total at the
returned predictor. The final candidate reads the cache instead. Tests check
both converged fits and deliberately exhausted one-iteration fits, including
inference='none', so this reuse cannot silently report an earlier state.

The initial new scalar-trial unit test compared against stats::binomial()$aic
with an unrecycled scalar vector. That R function's scalar logical mask used
only the first row. The test reference was corrected by explicitly repeating
the trial total, matching both the existing helper and the intended row
semantics. The initial test log and corrected passing log are retained. No
production calculation was changed in response to that reference error.

Validation and interpretation
-----------------------------
The fixed confirmation protocol is in protocol.txt. run.R records complete
fitting and derivative requests across small, ordinary, wide, large, and grouped
inputs, two links and two solvers, plus an ordinary logit control. It preserves
all comparison cases, iteration counts, solutions, and numerical differences.
The existing 146-case independent adjusted-score panel is rerun by run-stress.R
with its output redirected to this study; prior results are not overwritten.

This is established algebra and removal of repeated work, with empirical speed
evidence. No inferential theorem, finite-sample calibration improvement, or
uniform speed claim follows from it. The remaining native solver arithmetic
still has the cost of accurate tails; further speed work would need its own
mechanism and validation. Failed fits are not omitted or replaced in comparisons.

Reproduction
------------
From the repository root, install the baseline and patched candidate in separate
libraries. Regenerate exports using Rcpp::compileAttributes('.') in the candidate
source before R CMD INSTALL. Pass library and result-directory paths explicitly
as described in protocol.txt. Benchmark scripts refuse to replace raw timing
records. summarize.R rebuilds numerical agreement and timing tables from those
records. Reports and manifests record the completed artifacts separately from
the candidate/protocol hashes frozen before confirmation.

Final confirmation results
--------------------------
Across the ordinary n=12,000 probit/cloglog fits, complete fitting speed ratios
were 1.17–1.24 relative to c3b3eeb, corresponding to about 15–19% lower elapsed
time. The n=120,000 fits had ratios 1.14–1.24. Across all affected complete-fit
cases, ratios ranged from 0.945 to 1.240: wide probit QR measured about 6% slower,
and small probit QR was essentially unchanged. The unaffected logit control
also varied by solver, from about 4% slower to 9% faster. The measurements do
not establish uniform speed improvement or isolate all machine/order effects.

Derivative requests improved 1.10–5.93 times; at n>=12,000 the range was
2.32–5.93 times. All 22 paired fits had identical iteration counts, coefficients,
covariance matrices, adjusted scores, weights, and predicted probabilities.
Maximum relative deviance discrepancy was 5.64e-15, working-residual discrepancy
4.44e-16, and prediction-standard-error discrepancy 2.09e-17. Likelihood values
were identical in the stored comparison. The numerical comparison passed.

All 146 fits in the prior independent adjusted-score panel converged, with
maximum standardized adjusted score 8.02e-9. Focused binomial summary/tail,
Firth safeguard/cache/stopping, and prepared-fit tests passed. The prepared
Gamma worker test was skipped under the default CRAN flag in this focused run;
the package-wide validation records any separately executed worker checks.

Numerical solution objects retain every row used for the comparisons. To avoid
unnecessary repository size, archive.R losslessly recompresses their RDS byte
encoding with xz after timings finish and verifies identical R objects before
and after replacement. archive.csv records both byte hashes and sizes. The
benchmark runner is unchanged; regeneration can reproduce the original gzip
encoding, and archive.R performs the documented storage step. This changes no
timing, solver, numerical comparison, or statistical record.

The two complete solution records were reduced from 19,338,753 and
19,339,010 bytes to 9,917,688 and 9,908,876 bytes respectively. Both objects
were verified identical after recompression. validate.R verifies the complete
timing grid, numerical comparisons, stress results, and archive hashes without
rerunning models. Regenerate the final stress summary with:
  Rscript benchmarks/firth-tail-performance/validate.R RESULTS_DIRECTORY
