Actionable ordinal boundary diagnostics and local root investigation
2026-11-04; baseline 2619731

What changes for users
----------------------
An explicit boundary diagnostic request now locates the input rows and outcome
categories with the smallest fitted probabilities and cumulative-predictor
gaps. It reports observed category frequencies, total row frequencies, original
predictor values and offsets. Source results also identify the block and row
within the block. Raw scores, standardized scores and information conditioning
are shown with their coordinate conventions. Raw scores depend on predictor
units; the standardized values use the centered/scaled fitting coordinates.

Ordinary diagnostics still require no source reads. The explicit request makes
one source pass and retains only the requested number of rows in each of two
tables. Its extra memory is bounded by the current block and these compact
tables. Zero-effective rows are excluded. Globally collapsed categories are
listed separately, while row tables describe the effective fitted model.
Unreadable sources or missing retained data produce an explicit unavailable
result while preserving stored convergence information. A finite log probability
can distinguish probability underflow from an actual zero-probability boundary.

The targeted diagnostic tests passed 172 assertions. These cover all three
links, common and partial slopes, grouped and coded responses, source reading,
row identifiers, category reduction, zero frequencies, unavailable data, and
finite log probabilities and positive gaps beyond ordinary probability range.
Public forwarding and display were integrated and tested separately. These are
diagnostic extraction checks, not evidence about root existence or inference.

Bounded numerical investigation
------------------------------
The previous sparse 96-row fixture remains development evidence. No new input
data or inference simulations were generated. Its logit and complementary
log-log fits approach a boundary at row 12, where category 2 is unobserved.
The saturated-pattern no-interior-root certificate from the previous study
does not apply to this fixture.

At six fixed positive gaps from 1e-3 through 1e-8 for each link, the study solves
only the tangent projections of the original full-model adjusted equations.
The full global mean-bias adjustment is retained. Each of the 12 attempts was
bounded at 50 iterations; ten met the prototype equation target, and the two
smallest-gap attempts stopped with small residuals. Those failures are retained.
None of these constrained tangent solutions is presented as a full root.

An independent 80-decimal evaluator confirms the following final-gap values
in working coordinates. The normal multiplier is the coefficient of the
boundary-gap gradient in the Euclidean projection of the adjusted score.

 link       gap     normal multiplier   largest tangent score   largest raw score
 logit     1e-8        -0.08339490             1.33e-9                  0.1320673
 cloglog   1e-8        -0.56318622             1.57e-9                  0.8918831

Across the gap grid the normal multipliers approach nonzero values while the
standardized scores decrease approximately with the square root of the gap.
The expected information grows in the collapsing direction. Thus a shrinking
information-scaled residual and a small Fisher step can coexist with a nonzero
raw adjusted score near this boundary. This explains why merely increasing
precision or the iteration limit is not an adequate remedy for these paths.
It does not prove that another interior root is absent.

Finite boundary limit and its assumptions
-----------------------------------------
The derivation in local-boundary-limit.txt removes the one diverging category
term from the expected information and applies the exact rank-one inverse
identity. Under a single collapsing category with zero observed frequency,
positive remaining category probabilities and nonsingular remaining information,
the full adjusted score has a continuous finite boundary extension. A nonzero
exact extension would exclude roots in a sufficiently small neighborhood of
that boundary point. This is a conditional local conclusion.

The independent limit calculation at the two projected final points gives
normal multipliers -0.08339486 and -0.56318617. These agree with the local
continuation: the largest difference between the limiting vector and the
full 80-decimal vector at gap 1e-8 is below 1.6e-7. The other predictor gaps
remain above 0.079 for logit and 0.064 for complementary log-log.

Exact rational checks on the stored inputs establish only one collapsed gap,
strict positivity of every other gap, zero observed count for the collapsed
category, and three independent remaining predictor patterns. Those patterns
give positive-definite remaining information. The limiting score itself is
evaluated with 80-decimal arithmetic without a certified interval enclosure,
and the projected points approximate rather than exactly solve a limiting
tangent equation. Therefore this study adds no new production root-absence
certificate, solver change, or relaxed convergence criterion.

What remains unresolved
-----------------------
The existence of any interior adjusted-score root for the original sparse
logit and complementary log-log fits remains unresolved. A global no-zero
theorem would need to cover the entire admissible parameter polyhedron, or
an independent construction would need to supply a valid interior root.
Local continuation and failed starts do neither. The ordinary likelihood's
concavity cannot supply the missing theorem for the full mean-bias equations;
one cannot assume a scalar penalty for them.

The useful product outcome is a transparent, bounded diagnostic of these
failures. The bounded investigation stops here. Further work would need a
specific global argument or a new, equation-preserving mechanism, rather than
additional undirected starts. No claim about finite-sample calibration, universal
convergence, universal finiteness or performance speedup is made.

Sources and reproducibility
---------------------------
The primary mean-bias equations are from Kosmidis (2014), Improved estimation
in cumulative link models, JRSS B 76, 169-196, DOI 10.1111/rssb.12025,
equations (6)-(9). Sections 4.2, 5.1 and 12 were read for this pass. Section 12
describes finiteness supported by studied examples, not a universal theorem for
the present fully varying model. The local expansion specializes established
equations using direct calculus and linear algebra; no novelty claim is made.

Run fixed-gap.R, the pinned previous high-precision.py, summarize-fixed-gap.py,
and boundary-limit.py with the paths shown in their headers. Separate result
directories retain every attempt, final state, binary64 export, high-precision
evaluation and limit calculation. The development protocol preceded the fixed
grid; decision-log.txt records the two subsequent limit evaluations before they
were run. Source hashes, the immutable native-library digest and session records
identify the baseline. Earlier study evidence remains unchanged.
