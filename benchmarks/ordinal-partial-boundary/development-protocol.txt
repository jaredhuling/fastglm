Partial ordinal mean-bias boundary failures: bounded development study
Baseline 17c4a8a, 2026-11-04

Purpose and decision
---------------------
Determine why the retained sparse individual-response fixture approaches an
ordering boundary without convergence. The package needs reliable diagnosis
and a useful solver when an interior adjusted-score root exists. This supporting
investigation may justify a numerical repair or a more specific failure report;
it does not establish universal root existence, finite estimates or inferential
calibration. This is development, with any later confirmation kept separate.

Fixed statistical target
-------------------------
Keep the original threshold/common/varying coefficient coordinates, cumulative
multinomial likelihood, global expected information, and mean-bias adjustment.
Retain the package's score, predictor-step, conditioning and positivity
tolerances. No clipping, ridge estimator, changed penalty, reduced adjustment,
constraint relaxation or coverage-based tuning is permitted. Integer frequency
weights and offsets have their existing meanings. All positive-frequency rows
must have strictly ordered cumulative predictors.

Competing mechanisms and separating checks
------------------------------------------
1. Arithmetic: compare ordinary scores, global information and adjusted scores
   at the same coefficients using an independent direct formula, including
   arbitrary-precision arithmetic near the smallest gap. Distinguish probability
   cancellation from an ill-conditioned information inverse.
2. Optimizer: inspect the frozen iteration trajectory, feasible step fractions,
   score components, normal/tangent boundary directions and conditioning. Try
   independent root algorithms or equation-preserving coordinate preconditioners
   only as labeled prototypes. Use multiple declared starts and retain failures.
   An independently verified interior root falsifies root-absence explanations.
3. Statistical domain: identify which row/category approaches zero probability
   and whether it is observed. Trace a local boundary path with nuisance/tangent
   adjustment and examine the limiting normal score. Failure of an optimizer
   is not evidence proving absence of any interior root. A local obstruction
   does not establish a global theorem.

Bounded initial experiments
---------------------------
Reproduce the coded 96-row, four-category, two-fully-varying-predictor fixture
from test-ordinal-partial-source.R (seed 154005), with its weights and offsets.
Run all three links at the unchanged default tolerance and iteration caps 30
and 200, and compare dense/source behavior on the retained partition. Store
every fit, warning, iteration path, final coefficients and source/data hashes.
Then evaluate at most twelve selected states with 70-or-more-decimal arithmetic
and at most twelve bounded independent root attempts (at most 200 iterations each).
No timing claims or simultaneous benchmark jobs are part of this study.

Stop or revise the question after these bounded checks if they cannot separate
the mechanisms. Log any new candidate or adaptive branch before running it.
Do not relabel the inspected fixture as fresh confirmation. A proposed repair
or diagnostic requires a separate frozen confirmation protocol with fresh
deterministic interior, boundary and coordinate/representation checks. Native
production edits, builds and installation are coordinated separately.

Evidence handling
-------------------
Use new result directories. Preserve failed candidates and their reasons.
Record which assertions are exact algebra, conditional theory, empirical
numerical evidence, falsified, or unresolved. Read the primary cumulative-link
bias-reduction sources before attributing boundary behavior to a theorem.
Retain access locations and reading scope; do not infer a universal finiteness
claim from the published finite examples. No inference simulations are run.
