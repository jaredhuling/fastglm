Partial ordinal mean-bias boundary failures
2026-11-04; immutable baseline 17c4a8a

Finding and scope
-----------------
The retained sparse fixture exposes different mechanisms across links. Probit
has an interior solution and needs more than the test's 30 iterations. Logit
and complementary log-log approach an ordering boundary, where one unobserved
category probability vanishes and the information becomes ill-conditioned.
Accurate evaluation alone does not resolve their nonzero adjusted scores.
The existence of another interior root for those two fits remains unresolved.

A separate, exactly characterized class does admit a decisive diagnosis:
saturated fully varying cumulative-logit models with an empty intermediate
category within a predictor pattern have no strictly positive mean-bias-reduced
solution. A bounded-memory detector now identifies that class. This conclusion
uses an explicit affine reduction to an established endpoint-half-count result;
it is not inferred from a failed optimization. It does not apply to the original
96-row fixture, whose varying predictors have many distinct patterns.

Bounded development evidence
----------------------------
The frozen development protocol retains the original likelihood, adjusted
equations, coefficient coordinates and convergence criteria. It reproduces
18 dense/source fits, independently evaluates 12 stored states with 80-decimal
arithmetic, and attempts 12 independent roots from four declared starts per
link. All outputs and failures remain in separate development directories.

At the final dense states:

 link       minimum predictor gap   80-decimal scaled score   outcome
 logit             1.22146e-9              5.92380e-6          cap 200
 probit            2.26300e-2              9.59585e-9          converged at 56
 cloglog           9.30390e-14             1.04401e-5          search failed

The unchanged score tolerance is 1e-8. These are scores standardized by the
square roots of the expected information's diagonal entries, not raw scores.
The independent evaluator reconstructs probabilities, ordinary scores, expected
information and its inverse, and the global mean-bias adjustment directly in
Python Decimal arithmetic. It uses exact binary64 input values and 80 decimal
digits. No production probability or matrix code is reused.

For the final logit state, the maximum absolute error in the native adjustment
is 1.22e-8 in coefficient score units, and its standardized score agrees with
the high-precision result. Therefore cancellation does not explain that stall.
For complementary log-log, the adjustment error is 1.61e-4 in coefficient score
units at a gap near 1e-13. Its native standardized score is 1.30e-5, while the
high-precision value is still 1.04e-5. There is secondary numerical error, but
removing it would not satisfy the existing convergence criterion at that point.
Early states agree to ordinary floating-point precision. Probit's converged
state has adjustment error below 1.7e-15.

The collapsing interval is between the first two cumulative predictors at
row 12, whose observed outcome is category 4. It is category 2 that approaches
zero probability. In the working coordinates, the final logit score has maximum
absolute component 0.132, but its projection along the boundary has maximum
absolute component only 4.38e-8. It is almost normal to the boundary. This is local
evidence of attraction to a boundary, not a proof that all interior roots are
absent. As the expected information grows in the collapsing interval's normal
direction, a small Fisher step alone does not establish a vanishing raw score.

Independent Newton/double-dogleg attempts use the same original adjusted scores
and enforce ordering through a positive gap parameterization. The adjustment
is not recomputed as a different estimator in those nonlinear coordinates.
All four probit starts reach the same interior solution, with standardized
scores below 1.7e-13. All four logit and four complementary log-log attempts
stall near a boundary and fail the native acceptance criteria. Those failures
are retained; no root-absence claim follows from them. Nonconverged dense/source
paths differ near the boundary because aggregation and rounding affect the
search; the converged probit fits agree. No alternative solver is promoted.

Exact certificate and implementation
-----------------------------------
Assume a logit model with no common slopes, at least three categories, exactly
p_n + 1 positive-frequency varying-predictor patterns, a square full-rank
pattern matrix (1,-z), and a constant offset within each pattern. The map from
the original coefficients to pattern-specific cumulative predictors is affine
and invertible. Mean bias reduction is equivariant under this map, so the
adjusted equations separate into saturated multinomial equations by pattern.

For pooled counts y_1,...,y_K with total m, the unique closed-simplex target is

  P = (y_1 + 1/2, y_2,...,y_(K-1), y_K + 1/2) / (m + 1).

Any empty intermediate count forces a zero probability and equal adjacent
cumulative predictors. Thus no root exists in the strictly positive domain.
The original coefficients can remain finite, so coefficient finiteness is not
a substitute for positive probabilities or regular interior inference. The
complete algebra and primary-source applicability audit are in
saturated-logit-certificate.txt.

R/ordinal-partial-boundary.R implements this sufficient certificate. It retains
at most p_n + 1 exact patterns, offsets and pooled counts: O(p_n^2 + p_n K)
values, with no retained row arrays. It abandons the certificate when a further
pattern or an offset difference is observed. Grouped zero-effective rows,
including multiplication underflow, are ignored consistently with the fitter.
The rank check anchors before scaling and conservatively declines poorly
identified cases. It never approximately merges predictor patterns or offsets.

The public behavior is an early, explanatory error for certified cases, with
affected category labels, using existing dense/source preprocessing. It avoids
expensive futile iterations and prevents a boundary value from being presented with an ordinary
interior covariance. It does not pool outcome categories, clip probabilities,
alter coefficients or adjust a convergence tolerance. Other links, common
slopes, additional patterns and varying within-pattern offsets retain their
existing fitting behavior.

Fresh confirmation and limitations
---------------------------------
The separate ordinal-partial-boundary-confirmation protocol was fixed before
evaluating its three-pattern example. All 28 checks passed against the immutable
baseline. At three fixed interior points, the native adjusted score agrees with
an independent affine endpoint-half-count calculation within 5.8e-15. The exact
boundary targets have the required adjacent ties and map to finite original
coefficients. A counterpart with positive intermediate counts converges to its
known interior probabilities within 5.5e-12.

The checks also cover grouped/coded expansion, block partitioning, invertible
predictor recoding, zero effective frequencies, and all stated exclusions.
The corresponding targeted test file initially passed 26 assertions. Public
preflight integration and its dense/source tests are tracked separately from
the immutable mathematical confirmation.

Primary source: Kosmidis (2014), Improved estimation in cumulative link models,
JRSS B 76, 169-196, DOI 10.1111/rssb.12025. The full published text was read,
especially equations (6)-(9) and Sections 5.1 and 6.1. Section 6.1 establishes
the saturated cumulative-logit endpoint-half-count result; Section 5.1 explains
why adjusted counts do not justify generic likelihood concavity. The affine
pattern reduction here states the additional assumptions explicitly.

environment. The protocol snapshots preserve the pre-execution text; later
